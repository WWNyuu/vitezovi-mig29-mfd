Vitezovi MiG-29 MFD Pilot Manual

How to fly with the Vitezovi MiG-29 MFD add-on v1.7.8: one MFD with situational awareness, navigation, intercept, fuel, stores and RWR pages, on top of KRN MODS' MiG-29A(M) Cockpit Modernization.

Before you fly

You need two things installed before the add-on: the DCS MiG-29A Fulcrum module and KRN MODS' "MiG-29A(M) Cockpit Modernization" V1.0.3, unzipped into Saved Games\DCS\Mods\tech as KRN describes.

Download KRN's mod from its release page: MiG-29A(M) Cockpit Modernization on DCS User Files.

Install

  1. Close DCS.
  2. Unzip the add-on anywhere (not into the Mods folder) and double-click INSTALL.bat.
  3. The installer finds KRN's mod, checks it is V1.0.3, keeps a copy of every KRN file it changes and installs the add-on. If it reports a problem, it has changed nothing.
  4. In DCS: Options > Special > MIG-29AM, keep KRN's own MiG29 box ticked.

Remove: double-click UNINSTALL.bat. KRN's original files come back and KRN's mod works on its own.

If the installer says KRN's files are not originals (another KRN version, or the old all-in-one Vitezovi package): delete KRN's folder, unzip KRN's mod again, run INSTALL.bat again.

HOTAS binds: INSTALL.bat also adds every MFD and UFCP button of the add-on to the MiG-29's own controls: Options > Controls > MiG-29, category Vitezovi MFD (56 controls: POWER, all bezel buttons, BRT, DAY / NIGHT, SYM, the UFCP keypad, MRK / DIR / NAV, the STP switch and KRN's other UFCP buttons). A bind does exactly what a click on the button does. A DCS update or repair takes them out of the list again: run INSTALL.bat again (the start-up test then shows NO HOTAS BINDS on the UFCP LINK line); your own assignments come back with them. With DCS under C:\Program Files, right-click INSTALL.bat > Run as administrator for the binds. If the installer says it did not find DCS's install folder, open a command prompt in the add-on folder and run INSTALL.bat -DcsPath "D:\DCS World" (your DCS folder).

Updating KRN's mod later: uninstall the add-on first, update KRN, then install the add-on version made for that KRN version.

Test missions (installed to Saved Games\DCS\Missions):

  • MiG29_DL_Test_Flight.miz: four MiG-29 slots at Kutaisi plus dynamic spawn.
  • MiG29_RWR_Test.miz: air start with a full load, an SA-6 site and an armed Su-27, for the RWR and stores pages.

Power and start-up

The MFD runs on the jet's DC power and has its own power button: the top-left rocker on the bezel, labelled PWR.

  • On: press PWR once. It needs jet power (battery switch on, or a generator running).
  • Off: hold PWR until the "DISPLAY POWER OFF" bar is full (2 seconds) and it reads RELEASE TO SWITCH OFF, then let go: the display switches off when you release. Let go before the bar is full and nothing happens.
  • Hot start and air start: the MFD is already on.
  • Cold start: the screen stays dark until the battery is on and you press PWR.
  • UFCP: comes on with the Aircraft Systems Supply switch on the System Power Panel (battery or ground power on first). It takes a few seconds to come on and to go off.
  1. Shown while you hold PWR; the bar fills over 2 seconds. When it is full it reads RELEASE TO SWITCH OFF, and the display switches off when you let go.
  2. Let go before it is full and nothing happens.

The start-up (about 16 seconds)

  1. Screen test: a short flash, then a test grid with grey steps.
  2. Boot ROM: the display's own hardware check in small text.
  3. Software boot log: each check ticks in with its boot clock, then a loading bar.
  4. Squadron badge with the program version and a start-up bar.
  5. BUILT-IN TEST checklist: every line goes from IN TEST to GO (white) or NO-GO (red). The right column shows real values: DC volts, your latitude and longitude (the nav alignment), datalink tracks, SPO-15, stations and the number of cartridges. Bottom-centre SKIP shows the result at once.
  6. SELECT CARTRIDGE: see below. Then the MENU page.

NO-GO lines are information, not faults of the add-on: NAV / POSITION without a position, DATALINK RECEIVER without datalink packets (export off on a server, for example), GCI / BULLSEYE without a bullseye in the mission.

  1. Test mode and the add-on's version.
  2. Check box: tick = GO, red cross = NO-GO, amber square = running now, empty = still waiting.
  3. Result of each line.
  4. A NO-GO line (here: no datalink packets yet).
  5. The line being tested right now.
  6. Your position from the nav alignment, latitude and longitude.
  7. Summary: lines passed and failed.
  8. SKIP: show the whole result at once.

Power cuts

  • Under 1.5 seconds: the screen goes dark and comes back on the same page.
  • 1.5 to 30 seconds: the start-up runs again without the cartridge choice; your plan, route edits and steerpoint stay.
  • 30 seconds or more (engines and battery off for a rearm, for example): with PWR still on, the full start-up runs again as soon as power returns, cartridge choice included.

SA page

The SA page is a heading-up picture around your jet in the style of the F-15 SIT page: datalink contacts, ground threats, your route, markpoints and the cartridge's drawings, optionally over DCS's own charts.

  1. H1–H5: cursor to the nearest to 5th nearest hostile contact on the page.
  2. INTC heading tape (only while INTC runs).
  3. Your bullseye position.
  4. Range scale.
  5. Active steerpoint: TO, bearing and range.
  6. Your jet.
  7. Cursor box on a contact.
  8. SA-11 threat ring.
  9. Route waypoints.
  10. INTC meeting point (X).
  11. INTC readouts: heading, time and range to the meeting point.
  12. Bearing and range from you to the cursor.

Reading it

  • Colours: green friendly, red hostile, yellow unknown, cyan your flight. Lines from contacts show where they are heading.
  • Top left: your bearing and range from the bullseye. Top right: range scale and the active steerpoint (TO ...). An amber NO DL or DL OFF appears there only when the datalink is missing.
  • Bottom right: the datablock of the contact under the cursor (bearing / range from you and from the bullseye, altitude, type).
  • Red rings are ground threats seen over the datalink.

Cursor: jump to contacts with H1 to H5 (nearest to 5th nearest hostile) or NEXT.

EXP: zooms around the contact or SAM under the cursor (else the cursor point, else your jet) and follows a contact you picked. RNG + / - inside EXP zooms only the expanded view; NORM brings back your normal range, and the next EXP comes back at the zoom you used last.

Declutter (SYM rocker, bottom left): left click less, right click more, five steps: 1 FULL, 2 GROUP, 3 ALT only, 4 CLEAN (no labels, no route), 5 MIN (contacts and threats only). On 2 and 3, contacts of one colour on top of each other share one label ("3X 8 .81"), and a label that would cover another moves beside or under its symbol.

Markpoints: MARK (or the UFCP MRK button) drops one at the cursor, or under the jet when the cursor is off. Up to 5. They appear on FPAS as places to go home to.

Moving map: on by default; switch it in Options (see the last section). Charts exist for Caucasus, Syria, Persian Gulf, Sinai, Iraq, Afghanistan, Nevada, Mariana Islands, Marianas WWII, South Atlantic, Germany Cold War and Kola.

Map textures (v1.7.8): INSTALL.bat offers to join DCS's chart tiles of each map you have into a few big textures (it shows size and time per map and asks yes or no). With them the whole map shows at every range and the MFD page stays light. Answer no and the MFD uses DCS's own small tiles, only around where you fly; run INSTALL.bat again any time to build them, and again after a DCS update that changes the charts.

EWR picture (v1.7.8): servers that block export (the SA page shows NO DL) but send "EWR | Picture Report" and "EWR | Friendly Picture Report" text messages: the add-on reads those and draws their aircraft on the SA page, hostiles red and friendlies as friendlies, placed by bearing and range from your jet. Between reports they move along the reported heading and speed; a report is shown for 3 minutes or until the next one, and only while there is no datalink picture.

PICTURE and INTC

Both are advisory and live only on the MFD: they read what the jet and the datalink already know and never touch the autopilot, HUD, radar or weapons.

The contacts grouped into formations, up to five groups, nearest first. Each row shows the group, BULLS (from the bullseye) or BRAA (from you), altitude, size and type, and aspect: HOT, FLANK, BEAM or DRAG with its direction. The selected group's call is written out (for example "BULLS 045/62, 8000, HOT, HOSTILE, 2 SHIP F-16.") above a small north-up plot.

INTC intercept steering (SA page right 3)

Starts on the contact under the cursor, else the nearest hostile (or from PICTURE left 4). Press INTC again to stop.

  • Heading tape at the very top: put the green diamond under the centre mark to fly the collision course (CC). Amber at the end of the tape = more than 20 degrees to turn.
  • On the scope: a yellow X where you will meet, dashed lines from you and from the target to it, a white ring on the target (amber = track lost).
  • Bottom right (in place of the cursor datablock): target and aspect, CC and time to X, bearing / range / altitude, closure, and fuel at X (amber or red against your FPAS bingo).
  • TAIL CHASE: the target is faster and running, so there is no collision course; the diamond points at the target.

NAV page

NAV steers you along the cartridge's route: put the green diamond on the centre mark and you fly the command heading (CMD) that keeps you on the planned leg.

  1. Name of the loaded plan.
  2. Current steerpoint.
  3. Bearing and range to it.
  4. Direction and cross-track error.
  5. Steering diamond (CMD): fly it to the centre.
  6. Centre mark.
  7. Commanded heading.
  8. Route list.
  9. Time-on-target line.
  10. SEQ AUTO: steerpoints step on by themselves.
Left Does Right Does
1 PREV Previous steerpoint 1 LOAD Choose a cartridge, MSN or OFF
2 NEXT Next steerpoint 2 TOT Set a time on target (UFCP keypad)

Bottom 1 ADD adds a typed-in waypoint, bottom 2 DEL deletes the active steerpoint and bottom 4 EDIT opens the route editor.

Top block: the selected steerpoint and its note, bearing and range, time to go and ETA, the leg course (DTK) or DIR, cross-track error and the steering bar. Off the leg, CMD brings you back onto it at up to 45 degrees. All bearings are true (T) and match the SA page, HSI and UFCP.

Route list: the route you fly (the cartridge's route with your edits) plus its airfields, with bearing and distance from the jet and the ETA along the route. PREV / NEXT, AUTO and the UFCP STP switch step through every point, typed-in ones included.

Which steerpoint is active: whatever you used last wins between PREV / NEXT, the UFCP STP switch and the jet's NAV panel WP-A/D buttons. In AUTO the next point is taken when you are within 3 km or have passed abeam.

Time on target

  1. Right 3 PT moves the cursor (<) to the point.
  2. Right 2 TOT, then type the time on the UFCP keypad as HHMMSS (or HHMM, then ENT). Mission time, as on the cockpit clock.
  3. Set as many TOTs as you like; each leg between two TOTs gets its own speed.
  4. Fly the cyan caret (your ground speed) into the green box (speed needed). The THEN line shows the next leg's speed.

Fuel at the TOT point and at the last steerpoint turns amber when it is below bingo or reserve. Ground speed needs about 2 seconds after opening the page.

Member routes (RTE, left 5)

One cartridge can hold every flight member's route: in DCS's DTC editor, start a point's note with member numbers ("1 IP", "12 TGT", "1234 RV"; untagged = everyone). Tags are the digits 1–5, each once, followed by a space or the end of the note; 5 is the planner's X column. A note like "250 KTS" stays a name.

  • Left 1 to 4: member 1 to 4's route.
  • Right 1 to 5: add or remove that point by hand; the number in [ ] is its place in the route.
  • Bottom 1 / 2 page, bottom 4 CLR, bottom 5 ALL, left 5 DONE.

Picking a different route clears TOTs and your edits. DONE without a change keeps them.

Typed-in waypoints

  • Bottom 1 ADD = latitude / longitude entry; ADD again = MGRS; a third time = cancel. CLR deletes the last digit.
  • Lat / long: 13 digits DDMMmm DDDMMmm, for example 4155850415172 = N 41 55.85 E 041 51.72. N/S and E/W come from where the jet is.
  • MGRS: easting and northing in the jet's own 100 km square (shown on the line), 10 digits, or 4 / 6 / 8 digits then ENT.
  • The point goes in after the selected steerpoint and is flown to directly.
  • Bottom 2 DEL deletes the active steerpoint: a typed-in point or one from the cartridge.

Editing the route (EDIT, bottom 4)

The route you fly is your own copy of the cartridge (or mission) route. EDIT changes that copy with its own cursor, so the active steerpoint stays while you edit en route. The .dtc file is never changed: LOAD (even the same cartridge), a different route on RTE or a mission restart start from the cartridge again. A short power cut keeps your edits.

The centre tank hangs right under the jet. Amber = selected station. Under the picture: the selected station, gun rounds, chaff and flares.   • Left 1 ADD lists friendly aircraft (players first); pick up to 5 with the right buttons. Left 2 removes the selected member.

  1. Flight members on the right buttons.
  2. Status of the selected member.

RWR (MENU right 4): the SPO-15 picture on the MFD. It shows exactly what the SPO-15 shows: sector lamps, type letters and signal strength. It does not name the radar or aircraft that is painting you.

  • The main threat sits in its direction, nearer the centre the stronger it is, with its SPO-15 type letter (P Z H N F S).
  • Amber = main threat, red ring = locked on you, flashing ring and banner = missile launch.
  • Other threats show their direction on the outer ring.

  5. Other lit sectors on the outer ring. They carry a type letter only when one other type lamp is lit, otherwise "?".
  6. Main threat line: type letter, sector direction, power level (out of 15) and LOCK or LAUNCH.
  7. All type lamps that are lit.
  8. Directions of the other lit sectors (above or below for the main threat shows under these when the SPO-15 lights it).

UFCP buttons and the STP switch

Three UFCP keys belong to the add-on (their labels are changed on the panel) and the keypad types values for the MFD pages.

The UFCP is powered by the Aircraft Systems Supply switch (System Power Panel, battery or ground power on first); it takes a few seconds to come on and to go off. The add-on's lines on it follow the same power.

UFCP control Does
MRK Markpoint at the SA cursor, or under the jet
DIR (was DVR) Fly direct to the selected steerpoint from here
NAV (was FLT/MENU) NAV page; again = back
Keypad, ENT, CLR Values for HSI, BINGO, TOT and typed-in waypoints
STP switch With a cartridge loaded: step through its steerpoints

With a cartridge loaded the UFCP shows the add-on's steerpoint: line 1 "STP P3", line 2 its true bearing and distance, the same numbers as the SA and NAV pages. With MSN the STP switch stays KRN's own (mission route).

Data cartridges, drawings and the DTC Planner

A cartridge is a MiG-29 .dtc file in Saved Games\DCS\DTC, made in DCS's own DTC editor or in the Vitezovi DTC Planner. Only cartridges for the current map are listed.

  • One cartridge for both worlds: its 3 waypoints, 3 airfields and 3 RSBN channels drive the jet's own navigation; its route (any length) drives the NAV and SA pages.
  • Member tags: start a point's note with member numbers ("1 IP", "12 TGT"); see Member routes on the NAV page. Notes hold 7 characters.
  • Examples installed: VITEZOVI GCI NETWORK (intercept and escort routes for four members, ranges, FLOT and orbit tracks), VITEZOVI FLIGHT 40 (member routes), VITEZOVI ROUTE 20 (a 20-point route).

Drawings (.geo.txt)

An optional file with the same name next to the cartridge (DTC\<name>.dtc + DTC\<name>.geo.txt) adds drawings to the SA page. The DTC Planner's DRAWINGS tab writes it; Notepad works too, one drawing per line, latitude / longitude in decimal degrees, radius in km, # = comment.

Using the DTC Planner

The planner is the single file Vitezovi DTC Planner.html in the Vitezovi DTC Planner folder of the download. Double-click it and it opens in Chrome or Edge. It works offline with every DCS map built in, so you can pass the file on to squad mates as it is. It remembers the last cartridge you worked on in that browser.

The screen

  1. New (choose the map and a name), Open… (load a .dtc and its .geo.txt; select both files together) and Sample (the VITEZOVI GCI NETWORK example).
  2. Save cartridge: writes NAME.dtc to your Downloads folder, or NAME.zip (holding NAME.dtc and NAME.geo.txt) when the cartridge has drawings.
  3. Copy .dtc text: puts the cartridge text on the clipboard instead.
  4. SELECT: drag route points and drawings to move them; drag empty map to pan. Delete or Backspace removes what is selected, Esc deselects.
  5. POINT: click the map to add a route point after the selected one.
  6. RING / RING?: press on the threat's position and drag out the radius (the live radius shows at the bottom). RING is a confirmed threat (red), RING? a suspected one (pink, dashed). A single click makes a 20 km / 10 km ring.
  7. FLOT / BORDER / AREA: click corner by corner, then double-click or press Enter to finish (a line needs 2 points, an area 3). Esc cancels.
  8. Route filter: ALL, or only flight member 1–4's route.
  9. Map layers: F10, TACTICAL, RELIEF, or DCS CHARTS. For DCS CHARTS, pick your map's RasterCharts folder (for example DCS World\Mods\terrains\Caucasus\RasterCharts). It is read on your PC, nothing is uploaded.
  10. NAVAIDS: beacons, VOR/TACAN and RSBN on the map on or off.
  11. Tabs for the side panel: ROUTE, NAV, RADIOS, DRAWINGS, SA.
  12. Cartridge name: also the file name and the name on the MFD's cartridge card.
  13. Program P1–P3: a MiG-29 cartridge holds three programs. With "Same route, waypoints and nav aids in all programs" ticked they share everything; untick it to edit each program on its own.
  14. Route points: name (keep it short), the flights it belongs to (1–4), X, and altitude in metres. No tick = on every flight's route. X keeps a point out of all four member routes; it stays on ALL and can be picked by hand on the MFD's RTE page.
  15. Jet waypoints: the three route points the jet's own navigation gets (WPT 1–3), used when the MFD mod is off or in old-style missions.
  16. Legend of the line styles.

Mouse wheel zooms; + / − and ⤢ (fit the cartridge) are at the top right of the map.

Making a cartridge, step by step

  1. New: choose the map and type a name, then Create. A cartridge only works on the map it is made for.
  2. Route: pick POINT and click the route in flying order. Then name each point and set its altitude in ROUTE POINTS.
  3. Member routes (optional): tick 1–4 on the points each flight member flies, and check each route with FLT 1–4 above the map.
  4. NAV tab: airfields and RSBN (below).
  5. RADIOS tab: radio presets and ADF homers (below).
  6. Drawings (optional): RING, RING?, FLOT, BORDER and AREA on the map, then name them in the DRAWINGS tab.
  7. SA tab: check what the SA page will show.
  8. Save cartridge and move the .dtc (or both files from the .zip) from Downloads into Saved Games\DCS\DTC. Restart the mission, then pick the cartridge at start-up or with NAV page right 1 LOAD.

Save refuses to write a cartridge with an empty route or a radio preset out of range, and tells you why at the bottom of the window. To change a cartridge later, Open… its .dtc together with its .geo.txt. Cartridges made in DCS's own DTC editor open too.

NAV tab: airfields and RSBN

  1. Airfields ARD 1–3: the jet's three aerodromes.
  2. Runway to land on.
  3. The cartridge's approach channel field; -1 leaves it empty, as DCS does.
  4. RSBN 1–3: only the stations on this map are offered.

RADIOS tab: presets and ADF

  1. Same radio presets in all three programs.
  2. Presets CH 0–19 in MHz (100–149.975 or 220–399.975). The COM/NAV page shows them in flight.
  3. Same ADF in all three programs.
  4. ADF channels 1–4: outer and inner homer in kHz.
  5. FILL FROM: fill both homers from an airfield approach or a beacon in one go.

DRAWINGS tab

  1. Each drawing with its name: click the name to change it. The name is shown on the SA page.
  2. Delete the drawing.
  3. The .geo.txt file exactly as it will be saved (the same format as in the table above).
  4. Copy the text, for example to paste into a .geo.txt you edit by hand.

SA tab: preview

  1. At: view the route from this point, heading towards the next one.
  2. Range 20, 40 or 80 km.
  3. GEO on / off, like SA page right 1 in the jet.
  4. The SA page as it will look, heading up. Live threats from the jet's datalink are not shown here; in the jet they always stay on.

Options, settings and troubleshooting

Options (DCS Options > Special > MIG-29AM > "MiG-29 WW MFD"). They are read when a mission loads, so restart the mission after changing them. Keep KRN's own MiG29 box ticked: it switches the whole cockpit mod on.

Option Off / on means
Datalink Off: SA shows only your own radar, FLT is off, the export does not scan the mission's units (saves CPU)
RWR page Off: no RWR page and no launch alerts
Launch warnings Off: launches show as a normal lock, no banner
Moving map On: DCS's charts behind the SA page, MAP button to hide them in flight

Brightness: top-right rocker, left click brighter, right click darker. D/N switch bottom right: left click day, right click night (separate levels). Night uses its own softer colours; threats stay bright (night_palette = false in your settings for the day colours, dimmed).

Your settings (text size, keypad mapping, ranges, units, power and start-up) go into Saved Games\DCS\Vitezovi-MFD-Addon\settings.lua, one per line. The installer creates it, and updates and uninstalls never touch it. For example range_units = "nm", boot_skip = true (keeps the power button but skips the start-up screens) or power_mode = "always" (MFD always on). All setting names and their defaults are listed in Cockpit\Scripts\DL\dl_config.lua in KRN's folder; don't edit that file, because updates replace it. Restart the mission after a change.

Timing log: add perf_log = true to settings.lua to see how much time the add-on takes in your sim. Every 30 s one block goes to Saved Games\DCS\Logs\mig29dl_perf.log (the MFD, per page), mig29dl_export_perf.log (the export, with your frames per second) and mig29dl_hook_perf.log. Remove the line to switch it off; off, it costs nothing. On busy servers the export reads DCS's list of all units only every 2 s (the datalink packet still comes every second, contacts move on by their speed in between); the export log says when it switches.

Troubleshooting

Symptom Cause and fix
MFD stays dark on a cold start Battery on, then press PWR (top-left rocker)
UFCP stays dark Aircraft Systems Supply switch on (System Power Panel), then wait a few seconds
NO DL on the SA page No datalink packets: the server switches off export of world objects, or the Datalink option is off. Only your own radar is shown, plus the server's EWR picture reports if it sends them
No cartridges listed Cartridges are listed per map; the file must be a MiG-29 .dtc in Saved Games\DCS\DTC
No moving map Check that Moving map is ticked in Options (DCS keeps an older choice) and restart the mission; some maps have no charts. Map black at the long ranges: run INSTALL.bat and answer yes to building the map textures
Installer reports KRN files not original Delete KRN's folder, unzip KRN's mod again, run INSTALL.bat
Installer says KRN's mod has changed (newer version?) This add-on is made for KRN V1.0.3 and never writes over a newer KRN; wait for an add-on update, or reinstall KRN V1.0.3
Installer stopped with a message about antivirus, a file in use or OneDrive Do what the message says, then run INSTALL.bat again; it finishes the job (UNINSTALL.bat undoes it)
Anything else Logs in Saved Games\DCS\Logs\mig29dl_*.log; send them with your report

The SA datalink is a simulation built from the unit list DCS exports (sensor ranges, radar horizon, terrain): DCS has no real Link 16 for the MiG-29.
